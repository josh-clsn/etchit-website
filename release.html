What's new.

Latest builds of etch/it (the writer) and fetch>it (the reader). Cut 19 May 2026.

Mac · signed Windows Linux Android · signed iOS · private beta Browser extension

Click autonomi:// links from any web page.

The new fetch>it browser extension turns ordinary links on ordinary web pages into doorways to the Autonomi network. Visit a page, click an autonomi:// link, fetch>it opens on your computer and shows you what's there.

It also adds right-click → "Open in fetch>it" on any 64-character address you've highlighted, and an address-bar shortcut: type fetchit, hit space, paste the address.

What you need: fetch>it desktop installed (the extension opens it), the extension itself in your browser, and a working internet connection. That's it — no account, no sign-in, nothing else.

Install in 30 seconds — same steps for any Chromium browser:
  1. Unzip the file you just downloaded.
  2. Open the extensions page for your browser:
    • chrome://extensions  — Chrome & Arc
    • edge://extensions  — Edge
    • brave://extensions  — Brave
    • opera://extensions  — Opera / Vivaldi
    • about:debugging#/runtime/this-firefox  — Firefox
  3. Turn on Developer mode (top-right toggle on Chromium browsers; Firefox doesn't need it).
  4. Click Load unpacked — Firefox: Load Temporary Add-on… instead — and pick the unzipped folder (Firefox: pick the manifest.json inside it).

What changed

  • Click autonomi:// links from any web page. The new browser extension turns the address into a clickable doorway in Chrome / Firefox / Edge / Brave / Arc. See the box above.
  • One QR, every app. Same look, same buttons, same scan — desktop or phone. Add your own title before you share.
  • Save the QR. One tap saves a tidy little card with the wordmark and the address. Drop it in a chat or hang it on a wall.
  • Snap and etch. Take a screenshot on your computer and etch/it offers to put it on Autonomi — one click, no fuss.
  • Blog from your phone. Write a post in etch/it on Android and publish straight to the network.
  • Mac & Windows. Real installers, no terminal needed.
  • Mac signed & notarized. Double-click the .dmg, drag to Applications, double-click — no Gatekeeper warning, no right-click dance.
  • iOS private beta — limited slots. Email hello@etchit.io if you want one. Open beta when Apple approves.
  • Twelve places to visit. The demo city grew — books, images, music, video, games, a calculator, and four community-built pages.
  • Friendlier errors. If your wallet stalls or a page won't load, you get a sentence in English instead of [object Object].

Try this first

  • Install fetch>it desktop + the browser extension (links above). Open etchit.io/city. Click any of the entries — each one's an autonomi:// link. fetch>it pops open and shows you what's at that address. No web server is hosting that page. Sit with that for a second.
  • Or just scan the QR on the home page with your phone camera — same city, same content, opens in fetch>it on mobile.
  • Wander every spoke: a book in library, image plates in gallery, a piano track in album, a clip in cinema, the handler tour in lab, a quick arcade in game, the project explainer in brief.
  • Visit community and play through all four: battleships, hi/lo, saloon showdown, and the brand-new compound calculator.
  • Etch something of your own. Open etch/it, paste in some text or drop in a photo, hit publish. You get an address. Send it anywhere — the recipient clicks the link (with the extension) or scans the QR and sees the same bytes.
  • Open the same address on a second device. Same content, every time. That's the whole idea: the address is the thing.
